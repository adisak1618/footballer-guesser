## Village team

### Apprentice Seer
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Becomes the Seer when the Seer is eliminated. The Moderator informs her by shoulder-tap when the Seer is called at night (so other players don't notice the role-handoff in no-reveal games).
*Alternate:* In addition to taking over for the Seer, the Apprentice Seer targets a player every night, but only learns whether the target is a Werewolf after picking the same player twice. Extra bookkeeping for the Moderator, but gives the Apprentice Seer something to do while the Seer is still alive.

### Aura Seer
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Each night points at a player; the Moderator confirms whether the target has a "special" role (anything other than plain Villager or plain Werewolf). The more special roles in play, the stronger this role becomes. Always include at least one "aura" role on the werewolf side (e.g. Sorceress) so the Aura Seer isn't just a village team-finder.
*Alternate:* The Aura Seer learns whether the target is currently on the village team. Use only with at least 3 non-Villager roles in addition to the regular Werewolves. The Cursed, Doppelganger and Drunk appear to be on their **current** team.

### Bodyguard
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Each night picks a different player to protect (not the same player two nights in a row). The protected player cannot be eliminated that night. May not protect himself.
*Alternate:* May not protect the same player twice in the same game.
*2nd Alternate:* The protected target also can't be eliminated the next day.

### Cupid
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** On the first night points at two players (one can be Cupid himself). The Moderator wakes both Sweethearts so they see each other. If one Sweetheart is eliminated, the other dies instantly of a broken heart. Sweetheart status overrides other win conditions including Lone Wolf, Tanner and Hoodlum. Sweethearts may have any other role in addition to being a Sweetheart.
- Both on village team → win with the village.
- Both on werewolf team → win with the werewolves.
- On different teams → they become their own team and only win if they are the last two players left.

*Alternate:* Cupid is included as an extra card in the deal; after selecting Sweethearts, Cupid receives the leftover card and plays as that role for the rest of the game.
*2nd Alternate:* The Moderator (randomly or intentionally) picks the Sweethearts, so nobody knows who they are except the Sweethearts themselves.

### Cursed
**Team:** Village (switches to Werewolf if targeted)
**Mechanic:** On the village team unless targeted by the Werewolves; if targeted, joins the werewolf team instead of being eliminated. The Moderator should wake the Cursed each night to let them know if they have switched teams, and continue waking them in subsequent nights so other players don't notice the switch.

### Diseased
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** If the Werewolves eliminate the Diseased player, the disease prevents them from targeting anyone the following night.
- In a role-reveal game: the Werewolves do not pick a target the next night.
- In a no-reveal game: the Werewolves still pick a target the next night, but no one is eliminated.

### Doppelganger
**Team:** Village (then changes)
**Mechanic:** On the first night selects a player. While that player is alive the Doppelganger is on the village team and appears as a Villager to the Seer. When the target is eliminated, the Doppelganger secretly takes over that role (Moderator shoulder-tap to inform her); the Seer then sees her as her new role. If she becomes a Werewolf she joins the werewolf team.
*Alternate:* The Doppelganger instantly takes the target's role on night one (the original holder still has it too — both play the role). The Moderator must call the Doppelganger every night under this variant so she can use her new ability if any; if she has no special action, call her anyway to disguise things. This variant can tilt the game heavily either direction depending on the copied role.

### Drunk
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Thinks he is a plain Villager for the first two days. On the **third night** the Moderator wakes him and shows him his real role. The Seer sees the Drunk as his actual role even before he learns it himself. During setup, mix a Werewolf, a Villager and most or all of the other special-role cards together and draw one without showing the players — that draw is the Drunk's real role.

### Hunter
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** If the Hunter is eliminated (day or night), he immediately fires his weapon by pointing at any player, who is then eliminated. He may also choose to fire straight up for no extra kill. No one may speak while the Hunter chooses a target. If killed at night, he fires the following morning.

### Lycan
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Has a dormant strain of werewolfism and appears as a Werewolf to the Seer even though she is not. Best added to the deck at random; players should not know for certain if this role is in play.
*Alternate:* In role-reveal games, the Lycan is revealed as a Werewolf when eliminated.

### Mason
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** On the first night the Moderator calls the Masons to open their eyes and identify each other. No one in the village may discuss the Masons directly or indirectly; the "secret society" eliminates any player who does (Moderator's discretion — even oblique references like "Bobby knew Joe was safe, but Bobby isn't the Seer" count). A player eliminated by the secret society automatically loses, even if their team would otherwise win.

### Mayor
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** The Mayor's day vote counts twice; the Moderator silently double-counts it. Moderator wakes the Mayor on the first night so the Moderator knows who it is. The Mayor can either be dealt as a role card or elected by the village on day one (with re-election after each Mayor's death).

### Old Hag
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Each night places a "pox" on a player. The poxed player must leave the game area for one day — no participation in discussion or voting, and is also safe from the Hunter and any other daytime elimination. The Old Hag may not pox herself, and may not pox the same player twice in one game.
*Alternate:* After the initial target returns, that player chooses the next pox target, and so on (chain of pox). If a poxed player is eliminated at night, the Old Hag picks the pox target the following night.

### Pacifist
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Always votes to keep players in the game. Effectively a built-in vote against elimination, which slightly favours the werewolf team. Best added to the deck at random; players should not know for certain if this role is included.

### Paranormal Investigator
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** One night during the game, points at one player. The Moderator confirms whether **at least one** of (the target, the player to the target's immediate left, the player to the target's immediate right) is a Werewolf — but does not say which.

### Priest
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Once per game, may bless a player at night; the next attempt to eliminate that player (by any means) fails. May not bless himself. The blessing persists even if the Priest is later eliminated.
*Alternate:* Once per game, the Priest may instead learn the role of an eliminated player. This variant is only used in the No Reveal game mode.

### Prince
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** If voted to be eliminated during the day, he is revealed as the Prince and survives. The village then falls asleep immediately, as if the elimination had succeeded.

### Seer
**Team:** Village
**Balance:** **+7** (visible on card image, page 13)
**Mechanic:** Each night points at a player; the Moderator signals "V" for Villager or "W" for Werewolf. To make a Werewolf hit unmistakable the Moderator can use a more theatrical sign (e.g. raising both hands as claws and growling).

### Spellcaster
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Each night may choose one player to be muted for the following day (the muted player may not speak but may communicate any other way). May choose to mute herself, but may not mute the same player twice in a single game.
*Alternate:* Each night the Spellcaster **must** choose a player to mute.

### Tough Guy
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** If targeted by the Werewolves, the Tough Guy is eliminated the **following** night instead of the same night. Players are told that no one was eliminated on the night he was targeted. The following night may then produce two or more eliminations (the Tough Guy plus anyone else the Werewolves take that night). Moderator should wake the Tough Guy on the first night to know who he is in case he is targeted later.

### Troublemaker
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Once per game, may call for two eliminations the following day. Until she uses her ability, the Moderator wakes her each night to check.

### Village Idiot
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Always votes down (votes for players to stay). Best added to the deck at random.
*Alternate:* Don't deal this card at all — randomly designate a player as the Village Idiot without telling him. His vote is always counted as a down vote.

### Villager
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** No special ability. Find and eliminate the Werewolves.

### Witch
**Team:** Village
**Mechanic:** Has two powers, each usable once per game. May save a player who was eliminated at night, and may eliminate a player of her choice. Both powers may be used on the same night. The Moderator should call the Witch every night and ask if she wants to use either or both powers, even after she has used them, to avoid telegraphing.
*Alternate:* The Witch may only use one of her two powers during the game, but may choose which one.

## Werewolf team

### Cult Leader
**Team:** Werewolf-aligned (Cult)
**Mechanic:** Each night picks a player to add to the cult (picked players don't know they are in the cult). The Cult Leader wins if every player left in the game (not necessarily including himself) is a cult member. Other teams' win conditions still apply. The Moderator keeps a running list of cult members.
*Alternate:* If the Cult Leader is eliminated, the first cult member he picked becomes the new Cult Leader (Moderator shoulder-tap when calling the Cult Leader at night). The new Cult Leader continues to add members, and joins any previous Cult Leaders on a shared cult team. The cult team only wins when every remaining player is in the cult.

### Minion
**Team:** Werewolf (knows the wolves but does not kill)
**Mechanic:** No card is required for standard use. On the first night the Werewolves pick a player, who becomes the Minion and joins the werewolf team. The Minion learns who the Werewolves are, but does not wake with them at night. The Seer sees the Minion as a Villager. If the Minion has another special role, that role remains intact (a Seer Minion is possible — usually disastrous for the village). If playing with both Vampires and Werewolves, each team may have its own Minion.
*Alternate:* Deal the Minion as a regular role card. Show the Minion who the Werewolves are, and show the Werewolves who the Minion is. The card itself prevents the Minion from also holding another special role.

### Sorceress
**Team:** Werewolf
**Mechanic:** Each night points at a player; the Moderator gives a thumbs-up only if the Sorceress points to the Seer (no thumbs-down on a miss). The Sorceress does **not** know who the Werewolves are, and the Werewolves do not know who the Sorceress is. The Seer sees the Sorceress as a Villager.
*Alternate:* The Sorceress is shown the Werewolves on the first night. Significant boost to the werewolf team.

### Werewolf
**Team:** Werewolf
**Mechanic:** On the first night the Werewolves learn each other's identities. Every subsequent night they must agree on a single target to eliminate; if they argue for more than a minute they forfeit that night's kill. Werewolves may not target each other. Werewolves try to keep their identity secret during the day.

### Wolf Cub
**Team:** Werewolf
**Mechanic:** A Werewolf; wakes with the pack each night. If the Wolf Cub is eliminated, the Werewolves get to eliminate **two players the next night**.

## Neutral / independent

### Ghost
**Team:** Neutral
**Mechanic:** Eliminated on the first night. Writes down a 10-letter message from beyond, with the only restriction that he may **not** identify any player by name or initials. Each morning the Moderator reveals one letter (in order) of the message. Clues are open to interpretation, which energises day-one conversation.
*Alternate:* The first player eliminated becomes the Ghost but retains his original team. In a no-reveal game, players have to guess which team the Ghost is on.

### Hoodlum
**Team:** Neutral (own win condition)
**Mechanic:** On the first night indicates two players. Wins only if **both** of those players are eliminated by the end of the game AND the Hoodlum is still alive. Other teams' win conditions still apply, so the Hoodlum effectively needs the village team to win in order for him to win as well.
*Alternate:* The Hoodlum picks **four** players. If all four are eliminated while the Hoodlum is still alive, the game ends immediately and the Hoodlum wins instantly; all other teams (except possibly the Cult Leader) lose.

### Lone Wolf
**Team:** Neutral (wolf-flavoured, solo win)
**Mechanic:** Only wins if he is the last player standing, or if he reaches parity with the village by being one of the last two players with one **non-Werewolf** player remaining. Wakes with the Werewolves to choose a target each night. Best added to the deck at random; players should not know for certain if this role is included.

### Tanner
**Team:** Neutral (wins by being eliminated)
**Mechanic:** Wins only if he is eliminated. Other teams' win conditions still apply; the game continues after the Tanner wins.
*Alternate:* Only the Tanner wins if he is eliminated — all other teams lose. Generates angst and gives the Werewolves another bluffing angle (until the Seer checks them).

### Vampire
**Team:** Neutral (own major team)
**Mechanic:** A third major team in addition to Villagers and Werewolves. Each night the Vampires choose a target in addition to the Werewolves' target, but **the kill is banked** — the Vampire target is not revealed and not eliminated until the next accusation occurs. Vampires can't be eliminated by Werewolves, making them slightly more powerful than Werewolves.

With three major teams, Vampires or Werewolves must eliminate the other two teams before declaring victory. Vampires are used in experienced large groups, substituting some Werewolves so that total (Werewolves + Vampires) equals the Werewolf count of a normal game. Special roles that target Werewolves (Seer, P.I., etc.) may also target Vampires (Moderator's discretion), but do not distinguish between the two teams.

## Special / non-player

### Moderator
**Mechanic:** Used only to randomly select a Moderator. For experienced groups, add the Moderator card to a deck that already has one card per player besides yourself; deal cards and have everyone go to sleep. The player who draws the Moderator card runs the game starting that first night. Not a played role.

## Rules variations (pages 1–10)

### Role-reveal modes
- **Full reveal (default).** Eliminated player's role is shown.
- **Team reveal.** Only Werewolf-or-not is revealed; specific role is hidden. Moderator may need to wake roles every night to keep the day-call pattern consistent.
- **Team reveals (multi-team).** Reveal which team the deceased belonged to (Werewolf / Villager / Vampire / Cult etc.).
- **No reveal.** Nothing is revealed. Pushes the game toward bluffing; favours the werewolf team. Pairs well with extra special roles.
- **Variable roles / werewolves.** Players don't know which roles or how many Werewolves are in the deck. Mix in extra Villagers, then randomly discard the same number of role cards.

### Nomination & voting variants
- **Two-Player Accusations (Big Brother).** First two players accused-and-seconded go to a vote; ties eliminate both. Guarantees one elimination per day.
- **The Process of Elimination.** A player declares another "safe"; everyone who agrees raises a hand; the safe player declares the next safe player. Repeat until one player has no hand up — they are eliminated.
- **Secret Ballot (Survivor).** Players write down a name; most votes is eliminated; ties trigger a restricted re-vote.
- **1, 2, 3, Point! (German).** Moderator counts down and all players point at a target simultaneously; most-pointed-at is eliminated.

### Daily-variant table (2d6 each night)
| Roll | Effect |
|---|---|
| 2 | The Werewolves' target tonight becomes a new Werewolf |
| 3 | First player accused-and-seconded is instantly eliminated, no vote |
| 4 | Two eliminations instead of one |
| 5 | Indicate the player last chosen by the Seer |
| 6–8 | No variant |
| 9 | Same variant as the previous day |
| 10 | Process of Elimination is used |
| 11 | No discussion except for accusations |
| 12 | The next night, the target of the Werewolves loses his special ability instead of dying |

### Timer guidance
- Strongly recommended. The free **Ultimate Werewolf Timer App** (iOS/Android) is the standard tool.
- Timers favour the village team (Werewolves are easier to vote off under pressure) — compensate by tilting role mix slightly toward the werewolves (sum of card values > 0).
- First day should be the longest; ~30 s per remaining player as a guideline, shrinking as the village shrinks.
- Endgame: extend the final day or skip the timer; the last few players need real discussion time.
- When time runs out, the Moderator picks one of three resolutions: (1) day ends, instant sleep; (2) any accusation already on the floor is voted on immediately; (3) brief final accusation window with a short defence.

### Tilting the game
Every role card has a small number in its lower-right corner. Positive numbers favour the village; negative numbers favour the werewolves. **Keep the sum near zero** for a balanced game. The Seer is +7 (visible on the page-13 card art); use the role-card numbers as the source of truth for balancing custom scenarios.

### Other moderator-guide notes worth carrying into our app
- **Shoulder-tap protocol** — silent way to inform a player of a role change or new ability (Apprentice Seer becoming the Seer, Cursed switching teams, new Cult Leader, Doppelganger taking over). We need an equivalent **private-state-update channel** to each player's device.
- **Atmosphere matters.** Theme nights (Rick Thornquist's English Pub scenario uses English accents, the Slaughtered Lamb setting and pub Ghost) make the game memorable.
- **Use a timer for every game.**
- **Post-game discussion** — important for team-building runs; participants reflect on trust, suspicion, and which players flew under the radar.

## Design takeaways for our Warewolf app

1. **Role-reveal toggle as a host setting** — three modes (full / team / none).
2. **Voting modes pluggable** — v1 ships the base accuse-and-second-then-vote; the four official variants (Two-Player Accusations, Secret Ballot, 1-2-3 Point, Daily Changes) come later.
3. **Private-state-update channel per player** — the digital equivalent of the shoulder-tap. Required for Apprentice Seer activation, Cursed switching teams, Cult Leader succession, Doppelganger role-takeover.
4. **Win-condition resolver** — neutrals (Tanner, Lone Wolf, Hoodlum, Cult Leader) and Cupid's Sweethearts all override default team wins. Even within our curated 15 roles we already have 3 simultaneous win conditions (Village / Werewolves / Tanner) plus Sweetheart overrides — v1 needs a single resolver, not ad-hoc team checks.
5. **Day timer is configurable per scenario** — 5 min tight, 10 min default. Surface as a host preference and tighten automatically as the village shrinks (~30 s/player guideline).
6. **Balance the deck via card numbers, not vibes** — every role we add ships with an impact number; the host UI should show the running sum so custom scenarios stay near zero.
