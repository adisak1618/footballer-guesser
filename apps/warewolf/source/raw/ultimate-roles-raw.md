## P5110001 — Cover / Advanced Games

Title block: **ULTIMATE WEREWOLF / ULTIMATE RULES**

Sections on this page:

- **ADVANCED GAMES** — once basic Ultimate Werewolf is familiar, add new roles, tweak rules, no single right way to play.
- **ADVANCED GAME SETUP** — group discusses which roles to include; Moderator has final say but tries to accommodate everyone; use the Scenarios chart to pick the mix of Werewolves, Villagers and special roles, or build a custom mix.
- **MULTIPLE GAMES** — with 25–30 players, split into multiple simultaneous games. You need at least one Moderator per game and enough physical separation that the games don't disturb each other.
- **GAME END SCENARIOS** — when special roles are in the game, "Werewolves eliminated → village wins" and "Villagers ≤ Werewolves → werewolves win" are no longer the only end conditions. Other roles bring their own win conditions (e.g. Witch with powers left can still swing a game for either team). Moderator must plan for these, and especially for 3rd-party roles like Tanner, where the game may end early.

## P5110002 — Rules Variations / Role Revealing

- **RULES VARIATIONS** — variations are designed to be mixed and matched.
- **ROLE REVEALING** — the basic game has you reveal the role of each eliminated player. Variants below limit how much is revealed.
- **TEAM REVEAL** — moderator reveals only whether the eliminated player was Werewolf or not. Specific roles never revealed. Moderator may need to call special roles every night to disguise who was eliminated.
- **TEAM REVEALS** — reveals the team of the deceased (Werewolf, Villager, Vampire, Cult, etc.).
- **NO REVEAL** — nothing is revealed at all. Most favourable to the Werewolf team; supports lots of bluffing (e.g. multiple players claiming Seer). Pairs well with extra special roles since identities stay hidden.
- **VARIABLE ROLES / WEREWOLVES** — uncertainty over which roles (e.g. Lone Wolf, Village Idiot) are even in the game. Mix in extra Villagers, then discard a random set of role cards equal to how many Villagers you mixed in. Variable Werewolf count also helps the werewolf team.

## P5110003 — Voting Variations

- **NOMINATING & VOTING** — changing accusation/vote mechanics changes pacing.
- **TWO PLAYER ACCUSATIONS (BIG BROTHER)** — first two players accused-and-seconded go to vote. Moderator marks one thumbs up, one thumbs down. Players vote; most votes is eliminated; ties → both eliminated. Guarantees at least one elimination per day.
- **THE PROCESS OF ELIMINATION** — one player declares another "safe / definitely not a Werewolf". All other players who agree raise their hands. The safe player then declares the next safe player, repeat until only one player has no hand up — that player is eliminated.
- **SECRET BALLOT (SURVIVOR)** — players write a name on a ballot. Moderator counts. Most votes is eliminated. Ties → revote among tied players, players may only vote for tied players. Persistent tie → all tied players eliminated.
- **1, 2, 3, POINT! (GERMAN)** — moderator says "1, 2, 3, point!" and all players point at a target. Most-pointed-at is eliminated; ties → revote restricted to tied players. Eats time but lets players see who pointed where.
- **DAILY CHANGES** — daily variants keep the game fresh. Roll two d6 each night and apply the row from the chart on the next page; announce immediately.

## P5110005 — No Werewolves / All Werewolves / Moderator Guide / Atmosphere

- **NO WEREWOLVES OR SEER** — game with no Werewolves and no Seer. Players don't know; Moderator still calls Werewolves and Seer at night and picks a victim each evening. Game is talked about for a long time afterward; can annoy players badly but adds a new layer.
- **ALL WEREWOLVES** — really short scenario. Every player is a Werewolf. Send everyone to sleep and wake all Werewolves; awkward silence; game is over immediately and Werewolves "win".
- **MODERATOR GUIDE** — Moderator sets up the room, picks roles, explains rules, answers questions, leads the group. Ideally moderate one game as a player before running one. Job is to make every player feel critical to the outcome; you should be invisible during the day, helping with voting/issues.
- **ATMOSPHERE** — each Moderator has a style; richly themed games are exceptional fun.
- **RICK THORNQUIST'S ENGLISH PUB** — themed game set in the Slaughtered Lamb pub; everyone must speak with an English accent; roles include local Drunk and pub Ghost. Hand out English sayings ("I'll put the kettle on", "Bob's your uncle", "Poppycock", "I'm knackered, old chap"). Small bit of effort that creates a memorable Ultimate Werewolf game.

## P5110006 — Room Setup / Score Pad / Shoulder Tapping / Tilting the Game

- **ROOM SETUP** — keep the room quiet; limit the number of Ultimate Werewolf games per room (unless room is huge); chairs in a circle with space on either side; some Moderators prefer a table (or tables) in the middle for cards. Players who don't know each other should use name tents.
- **THE MODERATOR SCORE PAD** — helps track roles and special circumstances. List special roles in left column. On the first night, mark which roles are called each night so you don't forget. Other columns track victims and special events (cult members targeted, etc.).
- **SHOULDER TAPPING** — some roles require tapping a player's shoulder to alert them to a new role/power. Tell players up front that a shoulder tap during the appropriate call means they should open their eyes (e.g. passing the Amulet of Protection, telling the Apprentice Seer that she is the new Seer, telling someone they are the new Cult Leader).
- **TILTING THE GAME** — different roles have different impact on each team; this is shown by the number in the lower-right corner of each role card. Positive numbers favour the village, negative numbers favour the werewolves. Keep the sum near zero for balanced play.

## P5110007 — Explaining to New Players / Keeping the Game Moving / Night Moderator Behavior

- **EXPLAINING TO NEW PLAYERS** — Moderator should cover: (1) a village with Werewolves in it; (2) win conditions per team; (3) basic day/night flow; (4) rules during night; (5) rules during elimination; (6) the roles in this particular game; (7) eliminated players must be quiet and not interfere. After explaining, take questions before starting.
- **KEEPING THE GAME MOVING** — first game of the night runs slower than later ones. Don't add editorial opinions or hints. Some groups stall on day-one accusations; encourage "it's getting late" pushing. After a few warnings, let the village sleep without an elimination if needed; this nudges day two to start with action. If discussion stalls, a 5–10 minute timer (visible on iPad / laptop) keeps players moving.
- **NIGHT MODERATOR BEHAVIOR** — call roles in a consistent voice; walk around so players can't deduce who is being targeted by how long any action took. Try to avoid gendered pronouns when calling roles (it's a tell when only one Werewolf remains).

## P5110008 — Use a Timer (full page)

- **USE A TIMER** — strongly recommended. Dock to a timer app or the free Ultimate Werewolf App (iOS/Android). Timers add urgency and pace; games with timers feel better than games without.
  - Werewolves are easier to vote off during the day under time pressure, which favours the village team — compensate by adding higher-power village roles (or by tilting role mix so the sum of the lower-right card numbers is greater than zero).
  - Moderator decides how long to make each day. Don't go too long. With a timer set per day, the first day should be the longest, each subsequent day shorter (e.g. 30 seconds per remaining player as a guideline).
  - Towards the endgame, extend the final day (or skip the timer entirely) since meaningful discussion needs more than three minutes.
  - When time runs out, Moderator chooses how the day ends: (1) day ends and everyone falls asleep instantly; (2) any accusation already being defended is voted on immediately; (3) players get a few seconds to accuse and a short defense before vote.

## P5110009 — Timer App / Moderator Mistakes / Team Building / Post-Game Analysis

- **THE ULTIMATE WEREWOLF TIMER APP** — free; tablet recommended for visibility; has a help section.
- **MODERATOR MISTAKES** — mistakes happen. If you can correct it cleanly ("I made a mistake, undo that") without imbalancing the game, do so. Otherwise try to balance the mistake by tilting subsequent gameplay slightly the other way. If you forget to call a role at night, put players back to sleep and correct quickly.
- **TEAM BUILDING** — Ultimate Werewolf is a great team-building exercise when run properly. Pushes decision-making, logic, persuasion. Works in meetings, off-sites, workshops, training programs. Depends heavily on the Moderator's skill.
- **POST-GAME ANALYSIS** — important part of team building. Immediately after a game, separate players into their respective teams; each participant briefly recaps experience [continues on P5110010].

## P5110010 — Post-Game Analysis cont. / Prizes / Things to Watch Out For / Scenarios / Village of Visions (5)

- Continuation: don't let the special roles overshadow the plain Villagers. Discuss what they would have done differently, what mistakes were made, lapses in logic. Then have players discuss trust patterns: who did they trust during the game, why, who seemed suspicious, who flew under the radar.
- **PRIZES vs. JUST WINNING** — token prizes (gift cards, t-shirts, etc.) sweeten the pot and raise the intensity. Optional, at Moderator's discretion.
- **THINGS TO WATCH OUT FOR** — Ultimate Werewolf involves misdirection, bluffing and lying directly to others. This stays in the game; behaviour should not bleed into post-game relationships. Eliminated players sometimes get bored or frustrated — encourage them to stick around and watch.
- **SCENARIOS** — use the Scenarios chart on the next page to pick a quick role mix. For new players, use fewer roles than suggested. The values in the lower-right of each role card should sum near zero for balance.
- **VILLAGE OF VISIONS (5)** — tiny village. Has a single Werewolf who is also a Sorceress. In this scenario the Seer gets no first-night vision; Werewolf/Sorceress should. Roster: **3 Villagers, 1 Seer / 1 Werewolf/Sorceress**.

## P5110013 — Custom scenario notes cont. / ROLE CARDS intro

- Continuation of "Building Custom Scenarios": each Villager publicly states their role, leaving the werewolf team quickly exposed; you can get away with fewer plain Villagers if you don't tell players what roles are in the game. ## P5110014 — Apprentice Seer / Aura Seer / Bodyguard

- **APPRENTICE SEER** — becomes the Seer when the Seer is eliminated. Woken up by the Moderator (shoulder tap) when the Seer is called at night if roles aren't revealed. *Alternate:* in addition to taking over for the Seer, the Apprentice Seer also targets a player every night, but only learns whether the target is a Werewolf after choosing them twice — extra bookkeeping for the Moderator, but gives the Apprentice Seer a way to contribute without the Seer having to die first.
- **AURA SEER** — looks for players with special roles that are not plain Villagers or Werewolves. More special roles in the game → stronger this role becomes. Be sure to have at least one "evil"/aura special role (e.g. Sorceress) so the Aura Seer isn't just a village team-finder. *Alternate:* the Aura Seer learns whether the target is on the village team; only use this with at least 3 non-Villager roles in addition to the regular Werewolves. The Cursed, Doppelganger and Drunk appear to be on their current team.
- **BODYGUARD** — picks a different player to protect each night (not the same player twice in a row); that player cannot be eliminated that night. May not choose himself. *Alternate:* may not protect the same player twice in the same game. *2nd Alternate:* protected target cannot be eliminated the next day.

## P5110015 — Cult Leader / Cupid / Cursed

- **CULT LEADER** — picks a player each night to add to the cult (picked players don't know they are in the cult). Cult Leader wins if all players left in the game (not necessarily including himself) are part of the cult. Normal victory conditions for the other teams still apply. Moderator keeps a running list of cult members. *Alternate:* if the Cult Leader is eliminated, the first cult member picked becomes the new Cult Leader (Moderator taps that player's shoulder when calling the Cult Leader at night). The new Cult Leader continues to add members and joins any previous Cult Leaders on the cult team. The cult team wins only if every player left in the game is in the cult.
- **CUPID** — wakes on the first night and points at two players (one can be Cupid himself). The Moderator wakes both Sweethearts so they see each other. If one is eliminated, the other is eliminated as well (broken heart) instantly. Both on village team → win with village; both on werewolf team → win with werewolves; on different teams → they become their own team and only win if they are the last two players left. Being a Sweetheart overrides other winning conditions such as Lone Wolf, Tanner and Hoodlum. Sweethearts may have any other role in addition to being a Sweetheart. *Alternate:* include a Cupid card in the deck-deal; after Cupid selects his targets, he receives the leftover card and becomes that role for the rest of the game. *2nd Alternate:* Moderator randomly (or intentionally) picks the Sweethearts, so no one knows who they are except themselves.
- **CURSED** — on the village team unless targeted by the Werewolves; if targeted, joins the werewolf team instead of being eliminated. Each night the Moderator should wake the Cursed to let them know if they've switched teams, and (after switching) continue waking them so other players don't notice the change.

## P5110016 — Diseased / Doppelgänger / Drunk

- **DISEASED** — if the Werewolves target and eliminate the Diseased player, they skip targeting the following night (they got sick). If the game does not have role reveal, the Werewolves still pick a target the following night but no one is eliminated. If there is role reveal, the Werewolves do not pick a target.
- **DOPPELGÄNGER** — selects a player the first night. If that player is eliminated, the Doppelgänger secretly takes over their role; until the target dies the Doppelgänger is on the Village team. Moderator clearly indicates the new role to the Doppelgänger (shoulder tap as soon as she gets the new role). The Doppelgänger appears as a Villager to the Seer until her target is eliminated, after which the Seer sees her new role. If she becomes a Werewolf she joins the werewolf team. *Alternate:* the Doppelgänger instantly becomes whatever role her target is (the original holder of that role also keeps it). The Doppelgänger must be called every night if using this variant so she can take her special action if she has one (if she doesn't have a special role, still call her to disguise things). Powerful but can tilt the game viciously one direction or the other depending on the role she copies.
- **DRUNK** — thinks he is a regular Villager for the first two days; only on the third night does the Moderator wake him up and show him his real role. The Drunk is seen by the Seer as his actual role (even if he doesn't know it yet). During deck prep, mix a Werewolf, a Villager and most or all other special role cards together and pull one out without showing it to the players — that becomes the Drunk's role.

## P5110017 — Ghost / Hoodlum / Hunter / Lone Wolf

- **GHOST** — eliminated the first night. At the end of that first night the Ghost writes down a 10-letter message from beyond, with the only restriction that he may not attempt to identify any player by name or initials. Each morning the Moderator reveals one letter (in order) of the message. Clues can be interpreted many ways; makes day-one conversation much livelier. *Alternate:* the first player eliminated becomes the Ghost but retains his original team. In a no-reveal game, players have to guess what team the Ghost is on.
- **HOODLUM** — on the first night indicates two players. The only way the Hoodlum wins is if both of those players are eliminated by the end of the game AND the Hoodlum is still in the game. Normal victory conditions for the other teams still apply. The Hoodlum needs the Villager team to win in order for him to win as well. *Alternate:* the Hoodlum picks four players; if all four are eliminated while the Hoodlum is still in the game, the game ends and the Hoodlum wins instantly, and all other teams (except possibly the Cult Leader) lose.
- **HUNTER** — if eliminated (day or night), immediately fires his weapon by pointing at any player, who is then eliminated. May also choose to fire straight up, resulting in no additional elimination. No one may speak while the Hunter chooses a target. If eliminated at night, he picks his target immediately the next morning.
- **LONE WOLF** — only wins if he is the last player standing (or achieves parity with the village by having only one other non-Werewolf player left in the game). The Lone Wolf wakes with the Werewolves to choose a target each night. Best added at random to the deck; players should not know for certain if this role is included.

## P5110018 — Lycan / Mason / Mayor

- **LYCAN** — has a dormant strain of werewolfism and appears as a Werewolf to the Seer even though she's not. Best added at random to the deck; players should not know for certain if this role is included. *Alternate:* in role-reveal games, the Lycan is revealed as a Werewolf when eliminated.
- **MASON** — first night the Moderator calls the Masons to open their eyes and look for the other Mason(s). No one in the village may directly or indirectly speak of the Masons, or players who speak of them are eliminated that night by the secret society. If eliminated by the secret society, they automatically lose, even if they are on a winning team. Moderator should use discretion; better gameplay if the subject is never raised. Indirect references count ("Bobby knew Joe was safe, but Bobby isn't the Seer") — that's a clear reference to the Masons, so that player should be eliminated. Players will only make this mistake once.
- **MAYOR** — vote counts twice when voting to eliminate a player (Moderator silently counts the Mayor's vote twice). Moderator wakes the Mayor on the first night so he knows who it is. The Mayor can be a normal role card handed to a player, or the players can elect a Mayor on the first day; that Mayor stays in place until eliminated, at which point another Mayor must be elected.

## P5110019 — Minion / Moderator / Old Hag

- **MINION** — no card is required for standard use. On the first night, the Werewolves pick a player who then becomes the Werewolves' Minion and moves to the werewolf team. The player learns who the Werewolves are, but does not wake with them at night. The Seer sees the Minion as a Villager. If the Minion has another special role, that role remains intact (so a Seer Minion is possible, though that would be very bad for the village team). If playing with both Vampires and Werewolves, each team may have a Minion. *Alternate:* deal this card as a regular role, and show the Minion who the Werewolves are, and also show the Werewolves who the Minion is. The card prevents the Minion from having a dual role.
- **MODERATOR** — card used when randomly selecting a Moderator. For experienced groups, add the Moderator card to a deck that already has one card per player besides yourself, deal the cards, have everyone go to sleep — the player with the Moderator card starts moderating that first night.
- **OLD HAG** — each night the Old Hag places a "pox" on a player; that player must leave the game area for one day, may not take part in discussions, elimination or any other daytime activities (also safe from the Hunter or any other method of daytime elimination). The Old Hag may not place a pox on herself or on the same player twice in one game. *Alternate:* after the initial target returns, that night the target chooses the next player to pox, and so on. If a poxed player is eliminated at night, the Old Hag places a pox on another player the following night.

## P5110020 — Paranormal Investigator / Pacifist / Priest / Prince / Seer

- **PARANORMAL INVESTIGATOR** — one night during the game, the P.I. indicates one player and is told whether at least one of the players to the target's immediate left or right, or the target himself, is a Werewolf. Moderator does not say which of those players is a Werewolf, just that at least one of them is.
- **PACIFIST** — always votes for players to stay in the game. Nice boost for the werewolf team since it's one extra vote against eliminations during the day. Best added at random to the deck; players should not know for certain if this role is included.
- **PRIEST** — one night during the game, may choose any player to be protected by holy power; the next attempt to eliminate that player by any means fails. May not choose himself. If the Priest is eliminated after "blessing" someone, that player is still protected. *Alternate:* once per game, the Priest may learn the role of an eliminated player. This variant is only used in "No Reveal".
- **PRINCE** — if voted to be eliminated during the day, he is revealed as the Prince and is not eliminated. The village falls asleep immediately afterward as if the elimination had been successful.
- **SEER** — each night the Seer points at a player and the Moderator shows a "V" for Villager or a "W" for Werewolf. To be extra clear the Seer knows the target is a Werewolf, the Moderator may use another indicator (e.g. raising both hands as claws and making a "growling" face).

## P5110021 — Sorceress / Spellcaster / Tanner / Tough Guy

- **SORCERESS** — looks for the Seer each night (Moderator gives a "thumbs up" if the Sorceress points at the Seer). The Werewolves do not know who the Sorceress is, and the Sorceress does not know who the Werewolves are. The Seer sees the Sorceress as a Villager. *Alternate:* the Sorceress is shown who the Werewolves are on the first night — a significant advantage for the werewolf team.
